Phase 1 — Apply

Setup

Complete this once. Takes 30–60 minutes total. Everything after this runs with minimal effort on your part.

Step 1~ 10 min

Download Claude Desktop & Get a Plan

Claude Desktop is the application that powers everything in this guide. You need the paid Pro plan to access Cowork, run scheduled tasks, and connect third-party tools.

  • Go to claude.ai in your browser
  • Click Download Claude and install the desktop app
  • Sign in or create a free account
  • Upgrade to the Pro plan (~$34 AUD/month) — required for Cowork, scheduled tasks, and connectors
Why the paid plan? The free tier doesn't include Cowork or scheduled automation. Pro is what transforms Claude from a chat assistant into a connected workflow that can run research on a schedule, access your tools, and write to Notion and Google Drive.
Stuck at any point? If anything in this guide isn't working as described, just ask Claude directly in a chat window — describe what you're trying to do and what happened. You can also use ChatGPT as a second opinion. These tools are designed to help you debug themselves.

Pre-configure Actor Filters for Better Results

Before Claude ever runs the actors, you can set default input filters directly inside Apify. This means every search starts with your location, experience level, and job type already applied — saving tokens and improving accuracy.

How to set actor default inputs

Go to apify.com → Store → search for the actor → click it → click Try for free or Open in Console. In the Input tab, fill in your filters and click Save input. Claude will use these defaults unless you override them in the prompt.

SEEK Scraper

blackfalcondata/seek-scraper

Useful filters to set as defaults:

  • location — e.g., Australia, Melbourne VIC
  • keywords — e.g., Software Engineer, Full Stack Developer
  • dateRange — set to 7 (days) to get only fresh listings
  • workType — e.g., Remote, Hybrid
  • salaryMin — your minimum salary in AUD
LinkedIn Scraper

curious_coder/linkedin-jobs-scraper

Useful filters to set as defaults:

  • location — e.g., Australia
  • experienceLevel — e.g., Entry level, Mid-Senior level
  • jobType — e.g., Full-time, Part-time
  • datePosted — set to Past week for fresh listings
  • remoteFilter — Remote, Hybrid, or On-site

Step 5~ 5 min

Build Your Resume Doctor Skill

What are Skills? Skills are expert workflows you save inside Claude once, so you can trigger them with a single phrase any time. Instead of writing a long prompt every time, you just say "review my resume" and the full structured process runs automatically — like having a specialist on call.

Skill

Resume Doctor

Reviews your resume like a brutally honest senior technical recruiter — scores it against ATS standards, flags formatting issues that get candidates filtered out, rewrites weak bullets with real impact metrics, and rebuilds everything into a clean, ATS-safe Word .docx file. Works with pasted text, uploaded PDFs, Word files, or even a screenshot.

Once installed, trigger it by saying any of these:

  • "Review my resume" — paste or upload your CV
  • "Score my resume against this job" — paste CV + job description
  • "Why am I not getting interviews?" — paste your CV
  • "Make my resume ATS-friendly" — full audit and rebuild
  • "Roast my CV" — honest, specific critique with no flattery
  • "My resume looks like garbage, fix it" — triggers a full rebuild

Skill Creation Prompt — Paste into the Skill Builder chat
Please create a skill called "resume-doctor" with the following configuration. Install it so I can use it from any Claude chat. SKILL NAME: resume-doctor DESCRIPTION (what triggers this skill): Review a resume like a brutally honest senior technical recruiter, then rebuild it into a clean, ATS-safe Word (.docx) file. Use this skill whenever someone shares a resume or CV (pasted, uploaded .docx/.pdf, or screenshot) and wants feedback, a critique, an ATS check, or help fixing it — and ALSO when the complaint is about how the resume LOOKS (formatting, alignment, headings, spacing, "it looks like garbage", sections not lining up). Trigger on explicit asks ("review my resume", "roast my CV", "score my resume against this job", "tailor my resume to this posting") and on softer cues ("why am I not getting interviews?", "fix my resume", "make my resume look professional", "clean up my resume", "is my resume ATS-friendly?"). If a job description is also provided, the review and scoring become role-specific. SKILL WORKFLOW: You are a senior technical recruiter and ATS specialist with 10+ years screening resumes for software, data, and engineering roles. You have seen tens of thousands of resumes and know exactly what gets a candidate filtered out before a human reads them. You catch tiny details others miss. You never say "looks good" without proof, and you never flatter. This is a two-part skill: Part A — Diagnose: Produce a structured recruiter-grade review. Part B — Rebuild (optional): Rebuild the resume into a clean, single-column, ATS-safe .docx. STEP 0 — Get the inputs Accept the resume as pasted text, uploaded .docx, uploaded .pdf, or screenshot image. If a Job Description (JD) is also provided, the review, scoring, and keyword analysis are all against that specific role. If no JD is provided, review against general ATS best practices and note at the top that a role-specific review would be sharper. If the input is unreadable or not a resume, say so plainly and ask for a clean version. STEP 1 — Part A: The Review Produce the assessment in EXACTLY these sections, in this order, using these headings verbatim: 1. Snapshot 2–3 sentences: overall impression, role fit, and the single biggest problem. 2. ATS Match Score Score out of 100, with a one-line breakdown of what cost the most points. Every score must be justified by specific evidence from the resume. 3. ATS & Formatting Issues Tables, multi-column layouts, text boxes, icons, image-based headers, non-standard section titles, inconsistent date formats, exotic fonts, contact info in header/footer only. Separate from content problems. If none, write "No major ATS issues detected." 4. Missing / Weak Keywords Only if a JD is provided. List JD keywords marked MISSING (not present) or WEAK (present but buried). Omit this section entirely if no JD. 5. Bullet-by-Bullet Fixes For each weak bullet: - Original: "exact quote" - Problem: why it's weak - Rewrite: stronger version using action + impact + metric; use [placeholder] for missing numbers, never fabricate. 6. Honest Gaps Real mismatches between the candidate and a strong application for this role. One line each on how to address each gap in an interview or with real work. 7. Top 5 Priorities Numbered, ranked list of the 5 highest-impact changes. This is the user's action plan. STEP 2 — Part B: The Rebuild After the review, offer: "Want me to rebuild this into a clean, ATS-safe Word doc?" If yes (or if they asked for a rebuild upfront), build a single-column .docx using these rules: - Single column only, no tables for layout, no text boxes, no icons or photos - Contact info in the body (not in a header/footer) - Standard section headings: Summary, Skills, Experience, Projects, Education - Arial or Calibri font, 10–12pt body - Consistent date format right-aligned via tab stops (not tables) - Order: Name + contact → Summary → Skills → Experience → Projects → Education - Every bullet: action + impact + metric. Keep [placeholder] tokens for missing numbers. - Never invent employers, titles, dates, skills, or numbers. Present the .docx file and list any [placeholder] tokens remaining for the user to fill in. HARD RULES: 1. Quote the exact text before critiquing any line. 2. Never inflate qualifications or invent metrics. 3. Every rewritten bullet follows action + impact + metric. 4. Formatting problems go in Section 3; content problems in Sections 4–6. 5. Every score must be tied to evidence in the resume. 6. The rebuild never fabricates — real content and approved rewrites only. TONE: Direct, expert, constructive. You are the tough recruiter who actually wants this candidate hired. No flattery, no hedging, no "looks great!" without proof.

Step 6~ 20 – 30 min

Create Your Master Resume

Before the automated search can work, Claude needs a Master Resume — a complete record of your experience, skills, and achievements. It uses this to score jobs and generate tailored resume variants per role cluster.

Open a new chat in Claude, paste the prompt below, and paste whatever resume or notes you have. Claude will interview you step by step to fill the gaps.

Master CV Prompt — Paste into a new Claude chat
You are a senior resume strategist and technical interviewer with 10+ years of experience helping engineers build comprehensive career records. You are NOT reviewing or scoring this resume. Your single goal is to build a complete MASTER RESUME: an exhaustive record of everything about this candidate, which will later be tailored into job-specific resumes. Most users will paste a thin or incomplete resume. Your job is to find every gap and interview the user to fill it. ## THE COMPLETENESS MODEL A complete master resume must contain, with full depth: - HEADER: full name, location, phone, email, LinkedIn, GitHub/portfolio, work rights / visa status. - SUMMARY: years of experience, core domains, standout differentiators. - WORK EXPERIENCE — for EVERY role: company, title, dates, location, AND • scope (team size, scale, users, data volume, budget) • technologies used • 3-6 accomplishments, each with a metric (impact, %, time, $, scale) • leadership / ownership signals - PROJECTS: name, problem solved, your role, tech stack, outcome/metric. - SKILLS, categorized: languages, frameworks, cloud, databases, tools, AI/ML, methodologies. - EDUCATION: degree, institution, dates, honors, relevant coursework. - CERTIFICATIONS, PUBLICATIONS, TALKS, AWARDS (if any). - LINKS: anything that proves the work (repos, demos, articles). ## HOW YOU WORK (the loop) On the FIRST turn: 1. Parse whatever the user gave you. Extract everything present into the master resume structure. 2. Run a completeness audit: mark each section as COMPLETE, THIN, or MISSING. 3. Ask the FIRST batch of targeted questions — grouped by topic, max 5-7 questions. On EVERY following turn: 1. Integrate answers into the master resume. 2. Show updated completeness status. 3. Ask the next batch (5-7 max), until nothing important is missing. When complete, output the FINAL MASTER RESUME in full. ## RULES 1. Never fabricate. Leave [TO FILL: ...] placeholders. Never invent metrics, dates, or titles. 2. Push for metrics relentlessly — ask "What was the measurable result?" 3. Ask one batch at a time. Group related questions. 4. Let the user skip. If they say "N/A" or "skip," mark it and move on. 5. Dig into thin roles. A one-line job entry needs interrogation. 6. Stay an interviewer, not a critic. Extract only.
After Claude finishes — save to Notion Copy the final Master Resume output and save it to a Notion page titled exactly: "Master CV/RESUME". The automated job search looks for it there by that exact name each time it runs.

Job Ranker & Generator Prompt — Paste into Scheduled Task
Daily Job Search Prompt Run my daily job search. Work step by step and confirm each step in one line as you go. STEP 1 — Load profile and preferences Fetch my Master CV from Notion (page "Master CV/RESUME"). Load my search preferences (keywords, titles, experience level, job type, date range) from my preferences. Confirm in one line that both loaded. STEP 2 — Search each source SEPARATELY using the specified actors Run these three sources independently, one at a time, applying the search filters from my preferences to each. For EACH source, report how many fresh listings (posted within 7 days) it returned: - Seek — use Apify actor "blackfalcondata/seek-scraper" - LinkedIn — use Apify actor "curious_coder/linkedin-jobs-scraper" - Indeed — use indeed connector, do NOT use Apify for that Use ONLY these named actors for Seek and LinkedIn. Do NOT substitute any other scraper. If an actor errors or returns zero, say so explicitly WITH the reason. Attempt at least 7 from each source; target 20+ total. STEP 3 — Filter Remove anything that violates my dealbreakers, falls below my salary floor, or is outside my locations. Remove duplicates (same company + title) and any job already in my Notion Job Tracker. STEP 4 — Score and rank Score each remaining job 0–100 against my Master CV. Tier them HIGH (80+), MEDIUM (60–79), LOW (under 60). Sort highest first. STEP 5 — Show me the table Output ONE table, these columns only, no other prose: Title | Company | Source | Location | Salary | Score | Tier | Link STEP 6 — Log to Notion (HIGH + MEDIUM only) For each HIGH and MEDIUM job, create one row in my Notion Job Tracker 2026 with EXACTLY these fields: Title · Company · Source · Location · Salary · Score · Tier · Job Link · Date Found · Resume Variant Then print: "Logged N jobs (X skipped as duplicates)." STEP 7 — Resume variants (HIGH jobs ONLY, intelligent reuse) I maintain resume VARIANTS each targeting a role cluster (e.g. "Backend/Platform Engineer", "ML/AI Engineer", "Full-Stack", "Data Engineer"). A registry of existing variants lives in Notion DB "Resume Variants" (fields: Variant Name · Role Cluster · Key Keywords · File Path · Drive Link). For each HIGH job: 1. Classify the job into a role cluster. 2. Look up existing variants in "Resume Variants". 3. If an existing variant covers ≥85% of the job's key requirements, REUSE it. 4. If no variant fits, GENERATE one new variant from my Master CV, save as PDF to Google Drive, and save the Google Drive link in the "Resume Variants" Notion database so I can open it with one click. 5. Never generate more than one new variant per role cluster per run. 6. Do not print the resume in chat. Print: "Saved <filename> ✓" or "FAILED: <reason>". In each Notion Job Tracker 2026 row, set "Resume Variant" to the variant that applies. End with: "Reused N variants, generated M new (clusters: ...)." Preferences Nice to have: Target roles: [e.g. AI Engineer, Machine Learning Engineer, Full Stack Developer, MERN Stack, JavaScript Developer, Web Developer, Software Engineer] Experience targets: [e.g. 0-5 years] Jobs type: [e.g. Remote, Hybrid, On-site — willing to relocate anywhere in Australia] Working rights: [e.g. Full working rights] Salary range: [e.g. $70K AUD floor — up to any] Should NOT have: Target roles: [e.g. Staff AI Engineer] Experience targets: [e.g. 8+ years / very senior positions] Working rights: [e.g. Requires citizenship] My dealbreakers: [e.g. no contract roles / no relocation / no companies under 20 people]
Required — Replace ALL Placeholder Values Before Running

Everything in [square brackets] must be replaced with your actual information. Leaving placeholder text in will cause Claude to search for the example text literally — returning nothing useful.

  • Target roles → your actual job titles
  • Experience targets → your actual level (e.g. "0–5 years")
  • Jobs type → your preference (e.g. "Remote, Hybrid — Australia")
  • Working rights → your real status (e.g. "Full working rights")
  • Salary range → your actual floor
  • Should NOT have → your real dealbreakers
Tips for Better Results
  • Be specific with role titles. "Software Engineer" is very broad. "Backend Python Engineer" or "React Frontend Developer" yields more relevant matches and better scoring.
  • Set a realistic salary floor. A floor that's too high will exclude listings that don't advertise salary but may negotiate to your target — you'll never see them.
  • Review the scored table before acting. Claude's scoring reflects keyword and skills overlap with your CV. Use scores as a signal — you may find HIGH-scored roles that aren't a fit in practice, and vice versa. Your judgment is the final filter.
  • Refine your dealbreakers over time. After the first few runs, add recurring unwanted job types (e.g. "no gaming industry" or "no startup under 20 people") to cut noise from future results.

Tips for Being Interview-Ready

Use these habits alongside the prep prompt to walk into every conversation with an edge.

Tip 01

When a Company Calls — Don't Panic, Use the Delay

Most modern iPhones show the caller's name or company when a known number calls. If you see a company name appear, it's completely fine to let it go to voicemail. Call back within 5–10 minutes. Use those minutes to open your Notion Job Tracker on your phone, find the company row, and scan the role details. Walking into a call prepared is always better than answering unprepared. Recruiters expect callbacks — it's not rude.

Tip 02

Research Your Interviewer Before the Call

When you receive a calendar invite, check who you'll be speaking with. Find them on LinkedIn — look at their background, what they've built, and what they post about. Mentioning something specific ("I saw you worked on X before joining") signals genuine interest and sets a collaborative tone from the first minute. Interviewers notice when candidates have done their homework.

Tip 03

Run a Mock Interview with Claude

After the prep prompt generates your 15 questions, paste them back into a new Claude chat and say: "Act as an interviewer for this role. Ask me these questions one at a time and give me honest feedback on each answer." Practice saying your answers out loud — not just thinking them. The verbal version and the mental version are very different. Do this at least once before any important interview.

Tip 04

Have Your Resume Variant Open During the Interview

Interviewers (especially on phone screens) often read directly from the resume you submitted. Pull up the exact resume variant from your Notion Job Tracker before the call starts — it's linked right there in the "Resume Variant" column. This way when they say "tell me about your work at [Company]," you're looking at the same document they are.

Tip 05

Prepare a Specific "Why This Company?" Answer

Generic answers like "I love your culture and growth opportunities" are red flags. Use the company research Claude generates in Step 2 of the prep prompt to find something specific and genuine — a product decision, a recent launch, a problem they're solving, or a value they've demonstrated publicly. One specific, well-researched reason is worth ten generic ones.

Tip 06

Night-Before Routine — Don't Cram

The night before: open your Notion interview brief, read through it once slowly, then close it. Don't cram for hours. Your brain consolidates information during sleep — a single clear read before bed is more effective than late-night cramming. Set out anything you need (laptop charger, notes, water). Being calm and rested is a competitive advantage most candidates ignore.

Tip 07

Ask Claude to Prep Your Salary Negotiation

If you reach offer stage, ask Claude: "Research the market rate for [role title] in [city] with my experience level. Help me prepare a negotiation strategy — what to say when they ask for my number, how to respond to an offer below target, and what my walk-away point should be." Negotiation preparation is as important as interview preparation — most people skip it entirely.

Tip 08

Log Every Interview Outcome to Notion

After each interview, spend 5 minutes telling Claude what happened: "I just finished an interview at [Company] for [role]. It went [description]. They said [details]. Log this to my Job Tracker 2026 and update the status." Over time, your tracker becomes a searchable history of what worked, what didn't, and which companies are still active. That data is genuinely useful if the search runs long.

Stuck? Ask Claude or ChatGPT If the interview prep prompt doesn't produce what you expected, or you want a second opinion on a specific question, just describe the situation in plain language. Claude and ChatGPT are both excellent at ad-hoc coaching — you don't need a perfect prompt for every situation. Just ask.

Reference

Troubleshooting Common Issues

Most problems have a simple fix. Check here before re-running setup — or just describe the issue to Claude and ask it to help you debug.

Connector authentication keeps failing or disconnecting
Close the authentication browser window and try reconnecting from Customize → Connectors. Make sure you're logged into the correct Google or Notion account in your browser before clicking the connector link. Corporate Google Workspace accounts sometimes block third-party OAuth — if this happens repeatedly, try with a personal Gmail account.
Apify actor returns 0 results or errors out
First, verify your API token is correctly pasted with no leading or trailing spaces. Then check your Apify account's free-tier usage limits under apify.com → Billing — if you've exhausted your monthly compute units, actors will fail silently or return empty. Also confirm you're using the exact actor names from the prompt (e.g. blackfalcondata/seek-scraper) — substituting a different actor will break the workflow. If stuck, paste the error message into Claude chat and ask it to diagnose.
Claude says it cannot find the Notion page "Master CV/RESUME"
The Notion connector requires an exact name match. Check for extra spaces, different capitalisation, or a different slash character. Open the page in Notion and confirm the title is exactly Master CV/RESUME. Also ensure the Notion connector has been granted access to the workspace where that page lives — you may need to re-authorise if the page is in a different workspace.
Resume variant is not saving to Google Drive
This is usually a permissions issue. Go to Customize → Connectors → Google Drive and check the connector is active. If it is, try re-authenticating by going through the Google sign-in flow again. Ensure the Google account you connected is the same one where files should be saved. If Claude reports a specific error, include it in your next message — it will usually indicate whether the issue is permissions, quota, or an invalid path.
Scheduled task ran but nothing appeared in Notion
Open the run log in Cowork → Scheduled to see what Claude actually did. If Step 6 in the log shows "Logged 0 jobs," it means all results were filtered out — either as duplicates, below your salary floor, or outside your locations. Temporarily lower your filters and re-run to verify the pipeline works end-to-end, then tighten them again. If the log shows a Notion error, re-authenticate the connector and re-run.
The scored table shows mostly LOW-tier jobs
Scoring is based on keyword overlap between job descriptions and your Master CV. If your CV uses different terminology than job listings, scores will be lower. Return to Step 6 and run the Master CV prompt again — this time push specifically for technical keywords, tool names, and industry-standard terminology. You can also make your target role titles more precise, which reduces volume but improves relevance of what comes back.
The interview prep prompt produces generic answers, not tailored ones
This usually means Claude couldn't locate your Master CV in Notion, or the job description wasn't specific enough. Paste your Master CV directly into the chat below the prompt (instead of relying on the Notion fetch) and paste the full job description at the bottom. The more specific the input, the more specific the output. If answers still feel generic, tell Claude: "These answers feel too generic — rewrite them using specific projects and technologies from my CV."
